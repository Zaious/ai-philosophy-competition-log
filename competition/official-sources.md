# Official sources

Everything on this page comes from the organisers' own pages, read on 27 October 2026. The pages may change, so check them before relying on a date or a rule. Our reading of the rules, with the points that are easy to miss, is on a separate page: [rules-map.md](rules-map.md). What others have said in public is in [public-discussion.md](public-discussion.md).

## The facts, as the pages state them

| | |
|---|---|
| Organisers | Zachary Goodsell and Elliott Thornley |
| Prizes | US$3,000, US$2,000 and US$1,000 for the top three essays, and a further US$5,000 for creative methodology, to be distributed as the judge committee sees fit |
| What may be submitted | up to three original essays, each up to 6,000 words excluding the bibliography, and a methodology report |
| What the AI must do | the arguments and positions must be primarily AI generated, with humans giving corrective guidance |
| Anonymity | essays are anonymised for review; entries are not anonymous to the editor |
| Judging | on the quality and originality of the philosophical content; style is not graded; a panel of academic philosophers; judging is blind to methodology reports and authorship |
| Per entrant | a named author on at most three essays, and at most one prize |
| Disqualification | prompt injection means immediate disqualification and a ban from future competitions |
| Publication | all submissions and methodology reports are made generally available after winners are announced; finalists receive comments from the judges |
| Submission deadline | 31 October 2026 |
| Finalists announced | early December 2026 |
| Results announced | end of January 2027 |
