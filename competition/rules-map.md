# The competition's rules, mapped

A working map of the official rules and FAQ, written while preparing an entry. It is a reading aid, not a substitute: the authoritative text is on the organisers' pages ([competition page](https://www.zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition), [detailed rules](https://www.zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition-rules.html), [FAQ](https://www.zacharygoodsell.com/ai-philosophy-competition-faq.html)). Read as of 27 October 2026; the pages may change.

## The test the organisers give

> "Did the AI originate and develop all the substantive philosophical contributions that appear in the essay?" (FAQ)

Everything else follows from that question. Humans may "specify the task, construct the surrounding workflow, select among AI-generated outputs, and provide generic feedback."

## Permitted, in the FAQ's own examples

| Kind of involvement | Example the FAQ gives or implies |
|---|---|
| Scaffolds and workflows | argument-neutral agentic scaffolds; automated evaluation, search, scoring, testing |
| Selection | generate many essays and pick the best |
| Topic | choose a topic, question, debate, text or tradition |
| Thesis | specify a position to defend, if the position is not itself a novel contribution ("Argue for prioritarianism") |
| Generic method | "make the premises explicit", "look for counterexamples", "consult the relevant literature", "write an objections-and-replies section" |
| Material | provide a paper, passage, dataset or body of literature to analyse |
| Several AIs | have AIs propose, criticise one another, collaborate, or have one revise another's work |
| Feedback | say a section is unclear, underdeveloped, repetitive or insufficiently rigorous; then leave the AI to fix it |
| Checking | ask the AI to check literature, originality, quotations, references, formal reasoning |
| Constraints and chores | length, citation style, structure, audience; deleting material; mechanical formatting and submission |

## Not permitted

- giving the AI an argument to write up, or a draft to rewrite, expand or improve;
- supplying premises, telling it to draw a particular distinction, giving an outline that already embodies the argument;
- repeatedly steering through hints towards a human's pre-existing argument;
- writing or substantially rewriting passages; adding footnotes, examples, objections or qualifications;
- curating material so narrowly that the selection supplies an argument;
- feedback that contains philosophical insight ("This reply fails because it overlooks the distinction between X and Y");
- giving the AI a novel view and asking it to argue for it.

## Rules that are easy to miss

1. **Memory.** An AI with long-term memory or earlier conversations is allowed, "but not if that memory contains relevant philosophical ideas supplied by a human." The FAQ's remedy is to disable that memory or start a fresh session. General preferences and argument-neutral workflow instructions are fine.
2. **Chat logs.** Strongly encouraged, not required, but entrants "should retain the logs in case the organisers need them to determine prize eligibility". If the organisers suspect human ideas in a promising essay, they will ask for them.
3. **The methodology report is the eligibility evidence.** "Methodology reports will be used to adjudicate unclear cases." It is read separately from the essay and only after the essay prizes are decided; essay judging is blind to authorship and methodology.
4. **Publication.** After winners are announced, essays and methodology reports are published under CC BY 4.0. The FAQ warns: "Do not include private material in the methodology report unless you are willing for it to be published. Make any necessary redactions before submission." Chat logs are listed among the things a report should contain, and the pages do not say whether submitted logs are published too.
5. **Anonymity.** The essay and the report are anonymised for review. Entries are not anonymous to the organisers, and an entrant may have the published version anonymised.
6. **Autonomous agents** may enter, but every entry needs a human custodian who gives a real name and email. Prize money may have to be paid through the custodian.
7. **Prompt injection** (instructions placed in a submission to manipulate the AI screening) means immediate disqualification and exclusion from future editions. The screening prompts are not disclosed.
8. **Prior publication.** An essay is ineligible if published or under review at a journal, conference or workshop, or entered in another competition. Posting it on a personal site, blog, PhilPapers or arXiv does not count.
9. **Word count** includes everything except the bibliography. The methodology report has no limit.
10. **One prize per entrant.** At most three essays per entrant, but no more than one can win an essay prize.

## What the pages do not say

- what file format OpenReview accepts for the essay, the report and any attachments;
- how the word count is taken (whether hyphenated words and contractions count once or twice);
- whether submitted chat logs are published with the report;
- whether entrants may discuss an entry publicly before results.

An entrant who needs an answer has to ask the organisers.
